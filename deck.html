An artifact is a working app that Claude builds inside the conversation.

Not code you download. Not a thing you install. You describe what you want, it appears next to the chat, and you keep talking until it is right.

These slides are an artifact. So is everything I am about to show you.

Thirty seconds, and it is the important thirty seconds
Never paste student names or identifiers. That is the whole rule. If you remember one sentence from this, make it that one.

Everything I am showing you today contains zero student data. Every name and score in these demos is invented.

It is not just names. In a school our size, “the student who moved here from Guam in October” identifies a kid exactly as well as a name does. So do IEP details, medical details, and filenames like smith_jordan_essay.docx.

The vendor’s terms and your authorisation are two different gates, and only the district opens the second one. Long version, plus an email you can send Monday: README.md, the Student Privacy section.

The prompt — 1 of 3

One paragraph. Paste it and fill in the blank.

I want to build a small interactive web app that helps my students understand
[CONCEPT]. Before you build anything, interview me: ask me one question at a
time about what my students already believe about this, where they get stuck,
what they should be able to do differently after using it, and how much class
time they'll have with it. Keep asking follow-up questions until you could
describe the finished app back to me and I'd say "yes, that's it." Then build
it as a single interactive artifact.

No install. No GitHub. No skill to set up. A browser and a free account.

Say it out loud: ask me one question at a time. Without it you get eight questions in a wall of text and you answer two of them. With it you get a conversation — and the app comes out matching your class, not the internet’s average class.

The prompt — 3 of 3

It asks about teaching, and it knows when to stop.

I want to build a small interactive web app that helps my students understand
[CONCEPT]. Before you build anything, interview me: ask me one question at a
time about what my students already believe about this, where they get stuck,
what they should be able to do differently after using it, and how much class
time they'll have with it. Keep asking follow-up questions until you could
describe the finished app back to me and I'd say "yes, that's it." Then build
it as a single interactive artifact.
  • Four questions about teaching, not about software. Prior beliefs, sticking points, what they can do afterwards, how long you actually have.
  • A stop condition. “Until you could describe it back and I’d say yes, that’s it” is what keeps it asking instead of guessing.
