## What you're doing

You're going to rank the safety hazards in *our* shop — not the ones in a textbook — and then
check an AI's ranking against what you actually know from standing in the bay.

You will use AI on this assignment. That's expected. But an AI has never been in our shop. It
doesn't know which booth we have, which bay is missing a lift, or what's in the PPE cabinet
right now. Your job is to find the places where that shows.

**Turn in your prompt along with your work.** The prompt is part of the grade.

---

## The four parts of a prompt

Every prompt you write for this assignment names all four. You should be able to point to each
one in your own prompt and say what it's doing.

- **Verb** — the thinking move you're asking for. *Prioritize. Compare. Critique.* Not "write."
- **Format** — the shape of the answer. A table shows you where something's missing. A
  paragraph hides it.
- **Voice** — whose perspective the answer comes from. Change the voice, change the answer.
- **Context** — the real-world details. Who, where, what equipment, what limits. If you don't
  say it, the AI makes it up.

## Part 2 — Build your prompt

Fill in every bracket. Walk the shop and look if you have to. Vague answers here produce vague
output, and it will be obvious.

> **Prioritize** the five hazards below by how likely each one is to injure a first-year
> student in our shop. Give me **a ranked table with one column for your reasoning and one
> column for a specific control measure.**
>
> Do this **twice.** First as **the owner of a collision center who is judged on cycle time and
> insurer turnaround.** Then as **an OSHA compliance officer walking the shop during an
> inspection.**
>
> Context: students are ages [___], in a high school CTE program, in the shop [___] hours per
> week. Our shop has [booth type, number of bays, lifts, welder type, dust extraction — list
> what's actually here]. The PPE available to us is [___]. Do not assume equipment I haven't
> listed. These hazards come from tasks the students actually performed: [paste your Part 1
> log].

## Part 4 — Find what it got wrong about our shop

Find **three places where the AI was generally correct but wrong about here.** For each one:

1. What did it assume?
2. What's actually true in our shop?
3. What's the correct control?

Things that count:

- It recommends equipment we don't have.
- It assumes a different booth, a different welder, a different setup than ours.
- It says "use the lift" for a bay that doesn't have one.
- It recommends gear we do have — but you know its actual condition.
- It missed a hazard in our shop entirely.

Things that don't count: "the answer was too general" or "it didn't know our shop." That's the
starting point, not the finding. Name the specific thing.

## Part 5 — Your call *(150 words max)*

Name the single hazard you'd rank #1 for a first-year student in our shop, and defend it.

You're allowed to disagree with both voices. If you agree with one of them, explain what the
other one was seeing that made it rank things differently.
