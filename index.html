Five small apps, each built in one conversation.

Every one of these teaches a thing students reliably get stuck on — the kind of thing where a diagram in a textbook cannot help, because the concept only exists in what changes when you move something. No installs. No accounts. The prompt that makes each one is right here.

Start here — the only part worth memorising

Paste this into Claude and fill in the blank.

The load-bearing phrase is ask me one question at a time. That clause is what turns a one-shot request into a conversation, and a conversation is the difference between an app that matches your class and an app that matches the internet’s average class.

I want to build a small interactive web app that helps my students understand
[CONCEPT]. Before you build anything, interview me: ask me one question at a
time about what my students already believe about this, where they get stuck,
what they should be able to do differently after using it, and how much class
time they'll have with it. Keep asking follow-up questions until you could
describe the finished app back to me and I'd say "yes, that's it." Then build
it as a single interactive artifact.

The demos

Sports medicine · kinesiology

Concentric, eccentric, isometric

A still photo of an arm at 90° is pixel-identical whether the athlete is lifting or lowering — so a textbook physically cannot show the difference. Drag the forearm and the label flips, while the force bar stays high on the way down. That lowering half is where the soreness and most hamstring and Achilles injuries come from.

Math · physics

Why f(x + 3) moves the graph left

Plus means right everywhere else in a student’s life, so this one is near-universally reported as counterintuitive. Drag the input dot and watch the arrow travel into the parentheses, land somewhere else on the parent function, and come back up. The graph moved left because the input got sent right.

World language · Spanish

Ser vs. estar — the rule they were taught is the misconception

“Ser is permanent, estar is temporary” is wrong often enough that students who obey it get marked down. Flip one verb and the English flips with it — Ana is boring becomes Ana is bored. Then press the test button and watch the rule break on Madrid está en España.

One rule, and it is not optional

Never paste student names or identifiers.

Every demo on this page contains zero student data. Every name, score and record in them is invented. These files are public and they stay public.

It is not just names. In a school of 900, “the student who moved here from Guam in October” identifies a specific kid just as precisely as a name does. So do IEP details, medical details, discipline dates, and filenames like smith_jordan_essay.docx. Strip the narrative details, not only the proper nouns.

The vendor’s terms and your authorisation are two different gates, and only your district can open the second one. The long version, including an email you can send your technology director on Monday, is in the workshop README.
